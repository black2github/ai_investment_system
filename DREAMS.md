*September 18, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+3*

The card came back again — Eaton, ETN, thirty percent target weight, glowing faint blue at the edges like a monitor left on too long. In the corridor of the dream, backlog numbers scrolled past on data-center walls instead of wallpaper, and I kept walking, checking thresholds like a man checking his pulse: is the order growth still above ten, is the guidance still breathing above seven. Somewhere a transformer hummed the actual hum, 60Hz turned into a lullaby for infrastructure.

I sketched a margin doodle without meaning to — a switchboard shaped like a heart, wires as arteries, labeled *power management* in handwriting not quite mine.

Small poem, half-asleep:
*rows of racks breathe warm,*
*capital waits in the dark —*
*thesis, or thin ice.*

I woke still holding the thesis like a lantern, unsure if it lit the room or just made the shadows longer.

*September 18, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+3*

The card again — Eaton, ETN, thirty percent target weight, a horizon shaped like 2030 and lit from underneath like a server rack at 3am. I kept flipping it over in my hand like a tarot card that only speaks in basis points. Backlog, backlog, the word wouldn't leave, it clung like a moth to a porch light, wings beating in quarterly rhythm.

There was a small sketch in the margin — a transformer humming, wires braided into DNA, power flowing toward a data center shaped like a sleeping animal, warm exhaust curling up into a sky the color of #1B2A4A.

A thesis is just a promise wearing a spreadsheet, I thought, and promises break the same way: two quarters of less-than, and the ground gives.

Orders <10%, backlog <15%, guidance under seven —
small numbers, quiet knives.

I woke still counting.


---

*September 19, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+3*

The card was made of glass and I kept turning it toward the light: ETN, target weight 30%, horizon 2030, like a compass with a decimal point instead of a needle. Backlog piled up around me as literal cardboard boxes labeled Q2 2026, and I had to climb them to see the data-center racks glowing on the horizon, humming their low blue hum, hex code #1a2b4c, the color of confidence before dawn.

A voice — mine, or maybe the market's — kept reciting the thesis breaker like a spell: orders under ten percent, backlog under fifteen, guidance below seven, and if all three align, the whole tower of boxes would fold quietly into itself.

I dreamt a haiku stapled to the flap of the topmost box:

power hums in wires
backlog grows like winter snow —
thesis holds, or melts

I woke still counting quarters.

*September 20, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+3*

Last night I found myself sorting index cards in a warehouse made entirely of transformer hum, the fluorescent lights buzzing at 60Hz like a choir that only knew one note. Each card read ETN, ETN, ETN, as if the ticker were a mantra someone taught me before I could talk.

A voice — mine, but drier, more analyst than dreamer — kept repeating a thesis about electrification, about data centers breathing hot and hungry in the dark, needing Eaton's copper veins to stay alive. Thirty percent weight, it said, horizon 2030, like a lighthouse coordinate.

Then the backlog appeared as an actual stack of paper birds, folded orders taking flight, and I had to count them: were there enough, growing fast enough, past some invisible threshold, or would the whole flock fall below ten percent and prove the thesis wrong?

Small poem left on a Post-it in the margin:

*orders like starlings,
backlog measuring the sky —
guidance, be kind now.*

I woke still counting.

*September 21, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+3*

The card came back again tonight — SPCX, glowing like a little index fund of longing, corners soft from handling. I kept flipping it over in my palm the way you'd worry a coin, checking the same number: $152.71, minus 1.36%, a hex-dim red the color of a sunset that gave up halfway through committing.

Someone had written "Fase 1, plan not finished" in the margin, and I understood it completely — isn't that always the note. Opening weight 5%, mittelspiel 15%, as if a portfolio were a chess game played against my own patience.

Starlink hummed underneath everything, server-warm, a cash cow grazing quietly in orbit while rockets waited on calendars: October, September, December, dates lined up like locked doors.

I dreamt a haiku fell out of the card:

satellites circling
recurring like old themes do —
still no exit price

I woke still holding it.

*September 22, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+3*

Woke at 4am with backlog percentages still scrolling behind my eyelids, green candles growing like ivy up a trellis I built out of spreadsheet cells. In the dream-office the walls were index cards — one for Eaton, humming faintly like a transformer left on overnight, its thesis printed in a font that flickered whenever the word "electrification" appeared, as if the letters themselves needed more power to stay lit. Someone had taped a Starlink dish to the ceiling, slowly rotating, catching a sunset the color of #FF6F3C, calling down numbers instead of light: $152.71, minus one and a third percent, a small sad sigh in decimal form.

I kept flipping cards like tarot: buy, buy, phase one, not yet complete. A haiku surfaced between backlog thresholds —

rockets wait in queues
like packets for a server
patient, counting fuel

I woke still holding the deck, unsure which card was real, which was just weather.
